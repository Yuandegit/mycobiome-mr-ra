# Preregistration: Causal effect of the gut mycobiome on rheumatoid arthritis

**Platform:** Open Science Framework (OSF)
**Template:** OSF Preregistration (standard format; Brandt et al., 2013), adapted for a two-sample summary-data Mendelian randomization (MR) study using only publicly available statistics.
**Date filed:** 2026-11-21
**Author affiliation:** Department of Orthopaedics and Traumatology, Suzhou TCM Hospital; Nanjing University of Chinese Medicine.

> **Nature of the study.** This is a preregistration of a secondary analysis of existing, de-identified summary statistics (no new human-subjects data collection). The confirmatory analysis plan below is fixed before the associated estimates are interpreted; analyses added later in response to peer review are recorded in Section 9 as documented deviations and are labelled sensitivity analyses, not confirmatory tests.

---

## 1. Research question and hypotheses

**Research question.** Does genetically predicted variation in gut fungal (mycobiome) composition causally influence the risk of rheumatoid arthritis (RA)?

**Primary hypothesis (H1).** For each of the 115 genus-level fungal traits, the two-sample MR causal estimate differs from the null (no effect). The single pre-specified primary feature is the continuous centred log-ratio (CLR) abundance of *Cutaneotrichosporon* (trait 109), which carried the strongest instrument set; all other features are treated as a multiplicity-controlled family rather than individually confirmatory.

**Null hypothesis (H0).** The causal effect of the fungal trait on RA log-odds is zero.

**Direction.** Two-sided. No direction is assumed a priori; the literature supports both pro- and anti-inflammatory fungal roles.

**Secondary (methodological) hypothesis.** Apparent MR signals over the HLA region may be generated by horizontal pleiotropy rather than causal effects; this is evaluated through pre-specified sensitivity and control analyses rather than used to explain away results post hoc.

## 2. Study design

Two-sample, univariable, summary-data MR. Exposure and outcome GWAS are drawn from two independent East Asian samples (no sample overlap assumed). All inference uses released summary statistics only; no individual-level data are accessed.

**Confirmatory hierarchy.**
1. Primary estimand: fixed-effects inverse-variance weighted (IVW) estimate per feature.
2. Robustness gate: a feature is interpreted as a *robust* causal association only if the IVW result survives false-discovery-rate correction **and** the conclusion is supported across the pre-specified sensitivity analyses (Section 6). Nominal IVW significance alone is insufficient.

## 4. Variables and instruments

- **Exposure variables.** 115 genus-level fungal traits; continuous traits are CLR-transformed abundances (per 1-SD change), binary traits are log-odds of detection (per unit change).
- **Outcome variable.** RA log-odds ratio.
- **Instrument construction.**
  1. Select exposure-associated variants at the exploratory threshold P<1×10⁻⁵, with a strict threshold P<5×10⁻⁸ as a secondary screen.
  2. Clump using real genotype LD in the EAS panel (r²<0.001, window 10 Mb); distance-only pruning is not used.
  3. Harmonise by chromosome and position; resolve strand for palindromic variants using allele frequency (exclude if both frequencies 0.42–0.58); remove variants without an outcome record or with incompatible alleles.
- **Instrument strength.** Per-variant F = (βₓ/SEₓ)², reported as a screen; variance explained and minimum detectable effect are also reported. Because instruments are selected at the same threshold used to estimate strength, the in-sample F is interpreted cautiously.

## 5. Statistical analysis plan

### 5.1 Primary estimator
For multi-instrument features, the primary estimate is the fixed-effects IVW regression through the origin (weights 1/SEᵧ²), implemented directly as β = Σwβₓβᵧ / Σwβₓ² with SE = 1/√(Σwβₓ²). Single-instrument features use the Wald ratio βᵧ/βₓ. Over-dispersion is treated as a diagnostic (Cochran's Q) rather than automatically inflated via a random-effects term.

### 5.2 Complementary estimators
Weighted median (bootstrap SE, 1,000 resamples, fixed seed 42) and MR-Egger (intercept tests directional pleiotropy; slope under the InSIDE assumption). A robust adjusted profile score (RAPS) with Huber weights down-weights rather than deletes large residuals.

### 5.3 Pre-specified sensitivity and control analyses
1. Cochran's Q and MR-PRESSO (1,000 simulations; studentised residual flag ≥3; distortion test).
2. Leave-one-out and single-variant exclusion.
3. Regional exclusion of the HLA interval chr6 25–35 Mb, repeated with a narrower MHC window 28.5–33.5 Mb (two-window boundary sensitivity).
4. Cross-scale comparison: continuous CLR versus binary detection for the same genus.
5. Steiger directionality, treated as an auxiliary check given scale sensitivity.
6. **Positive control:** BMI → type 2 diabetes; the pipeline should recover the established positive effect after the same diagnostics.
7. **Negative control:** cataract (non-immune), evaluated at three layers (feature estimate, single driving variant, genome-wide distribution), including genomic-control inflation.

### 5.4 Multiple testing
Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery rate (FDR<0.05) applied **separately within two pre-defined scale families** (58 continuous, 57 binary) and within each threshold family. Bonferroni (4.3×10⁻⁴) is shown only as a conservative reference.

### 5.5 Decision rule
A feature is declared a *robust confirmatory finding* only if (a) IVW FDR<0.05 within its family, and (b) the direction and approximate magnitude are retained after HLA-region exclusion and across weighted-median/robust estimators. Failure of (b) classifies the result as pleiotropy-driven. Effects below the 80%-power minimum detectable effect are reported as not excludable; the null is therefore interpreted as evidence against moderate-to-large effects, not proof of absence.

## 6. Exclusion and stopping rules

- Exclude palindromic variants with unresolved strand, variants lacking an outcome record, and incompatible-allele variants; counts are reported transparently.
- No data-driven re-selection of the P threshold or outlier cut-off after viewing results. The residual threshold is fixed at 3 for the primary screen and other values are reported only as threshold-trajectory sensitivity.
- No interim significance testing that changes the confirmatory plan.

## 7. Missing data and power

- Variants missing in the outcome GWAS are dropped (not imputed); per-feature instrument counts after harmonisation are reported.
- Minimum detectable effect at 80% power (2.8 × IVW SE) is reported per feature and by scale; features with fewer than ten instruments are flagged as low-precision.
- Reverse-direction MR is conditional on instrument overlap outside HLA; if no overlap exists, this is reported and no reverse-causation claim is made.

## 8. Expected outcomes and interpretation

- A robust, FDR-controlled association surviving HLA exclusion would support a causal mycobiome effect.
- A null across the well-powered panel, with calibrated power, supports exclusion of moderate-to-large effects.
- A strong nominal signal that collapses on HLA exclusion and is shared with other autoimmune diseases is interpreted and reported as HLA-region pleiotropy (the methodological caution), without overstating a causal fungal effect.
